Asteroid Impact Damage to a Coastal City
November 8, 2021
Team 216, Problem B
Abstract
In this paper, we find the minimum mass of an asteroid required to cause substantial
damage to a coastal city from an impact on an oceanic surface 1, 000km away. With a
range of asteroid masses, we investigate the final impact velocity of an asteroid when it
hits the surface of the ocean with an initial starting velocity at atmospheric entry. In
addition, the total energy lost due to air friction during this interval is determined to
better quantify the energy efficiency of the asteroid’s impact. From the impact velocity,
equations describing the cavitation and resulting wave propagation in the ocean after
the collision are used to find the final wave height once they reach the coastal city.
When an asteroid above a certain mass impacts the ocean, the waves reaching the city
amounts to the damage caused by a tsunami. Using data relating tsunami height and
structural damage, the threshold for substantial damage from the asteroid impact is
defined to be a wave with a height that is greater than or equal to 2m when reaching
the city. From this threshold and the calculated wave heights (for different asteroid
masses) 1, 000km away from the impact center, we find that the minimum mass of the
asteroid must be 109kg to cause substantial damage.

Problem Statement
The problem is to find the minimum mass of an asteroid required to cause substantial
damage to a coastal city from an impact on the oceanic surface 1, 000km away. From this
problem, we seek to model an asteroid for a range of masses that enters Earth’s atmosphere,
impacts the oceanic surface, and produces oceanic waves that propagate from the impact
center to the city. We find the characteristics of the waves once they reaches the coastal city,
1, 000km from the impact center, and determine if they cause substantial damage to the city
in their potential to destroy or partially destroy wooden or reinforced concrete structures.
For this problem, thermal radiation and wind currents generated from the impact are
not considered for three reasons.
1. Oceanic waves are very likely to be the first to have a significant impact on the coastal
city as the energy transferred to the ocean is large in comparison to that in heating or
generating wind currents.
1

Team 216
2. From the thermal conductivity of air and water, the mediums are considered to be
insulating. At a distance 1, 000km away, significant thermal changes in the city are
unlikely to be made on the timescale of a day.
3. Due to the large volume of air from the center of the impact to the coastal city, wind
currents generated from the impact are unlikely to have a significant effect in terms of
energy due to large dissipation.
2
Assumptions
• The asteroid is perfectly spherical. This assumption is made due to the wide variability
in the shapes of asteroid, leading to an over-complex model with likely insignificant
changes in the final result.
• The asteroid has a uniform density distribution. This assumption is made since the
density within asteroids vary but the center of mass would be approximately centered
in the body.
• The asteroid impact is in the vertical direction. This assumption is made due to range
of angles that the asteroid can impact the ocean surface, simplifying the computation
to a vertical impact.
• The sea floor is flat from the center of the impact to the city. This assumption is made
to make the model more general to different impact locations as sea floor terrains vary
by location.
• The incline from the sea floor to the city is modeled vertically. This assumption is
made to simplify the model without a significant decline in accuracy as the 1, 000km
distance from the impact center to the coastal city is likely to be large in comparison
to sea terrain changes near the shore.

Discussion
The steps that were taken to reach out conclusion is split into two parts.
1. Asteroid Impact
(a) The strengths of performing the calculations for impact velocity is that a time
varying ordinary differential equation is used. It was not assumed that the velocity
at the entry of the atmosphere was the the same as the velocity of the impact due
to the presence of gravitational and drag forces. This led to greater accuracy in
the final impact velocity determination for the range of masses used.
(b) However, the weakness of this approach is that finding numerical solutions (MAT-
LAB ode45 solver) to the differential equation does not give the exact solution,
only an approximation.
2. Oceanic Wave Propagation
(a) The strengths of performing the calculations for the wave amplitude is that the
solutions are directly computed from the impact energy of the asteroid and no
further approximations to solutions had to be made.
(b) However, the weakness of this approach is that the wave amplitude equation is
dependent on the assumption that the impact is in the deep water impact regime
and the effect of the sea floor is neglected. In addition, there is a slope from the
sea floor to the shore that increases the amplitude of the wave as the water depth
decreases. To obtain more accurate wave heights in future work, Green’s law can
be used and the slope of the rise can be included in the model.
